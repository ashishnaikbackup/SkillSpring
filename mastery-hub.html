02 · THE MASTERY METHOD

The six-step learning loop

Repeat this loop for every important concept. Do not move on just because a video or lesson ended; move on when you can explain and use the idea.

Step 1 · Orient

Know the destination

Write what you want to do, why it matters, the prerequisites and one real task where the skill is used.

Step 2 · Understand

Build the mental model

Learn vocabulary, principles and the simplest example. Ask what, why, when and what changes if an assumption changes.

Step 3 · Retrieve

Recall without notes

Close the lesson. Explain the idea from memory, sketch it or solve a small question. Check gaps only after trying.

Step 4 · Practise

Do the work yourself

Start with a guided example, then change inputs, solve a similar task and finally attempt a new problem independently.

Step 5 · Build

Combine skills

Create a small project with real constraints, test edge cases, document decisions and ask someone to review it.

Step 6 · Demonstrate

Prove your ability

Present the result, explain trade-offs, answer questions and improve it from feedback. Save evidence in a portfolio.

03 · SPACED REVISION

Remember more with active recall

Add a concept after learning it. Review it by trying to recall or solve it before revealing your notes. Suggested intervals are 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days; move a card forward only when recall is reasonably strong.

06 · PROFESSIONAL EVIDENCE

Use the mastery gate before calling a skill “learned”

Score yourself honestly. Aim for evidence at level 3 or above on every dimension before presenting a project as portfolio-ready. Ask a knowledgeable person to review it whenever possible.

1Recognise terms with help
2Explain basics and follow examples
3Complete a standard task independently
4Solve unfamiliar problems and justify choices
5Teach, review and improve real work

Evidence checklist

Portfolio quality checklist

  • Problem statement and intended user
  • Clear scope, assumptions and requirements
  • Working artefact, analysis or demonstration
  • Tests, sources, calculations or validation
  • Readable README / report / case study
  • What you learned and what you would improve
  • Honest statement of your contribution

Never publish private data, credentials, confidential employer material or another person's work as your own.

Universal project brief

Pick a problem in your school. Define who experiences it, what success means, what you need to learn, what you will build or analyse, how you will test it, and how you will communicate the result. Start small, then add one meaningful professional feature at a time.
