2. Build a weekly study plan

Choose a sustainable number of hours. The planner distributes your time across new learning, active practice, revision and portfolio work.

A useful 60-minute session: 5 min recall yesterday's ideas · 20 min learn one small concept · 25 min solve/build without copying · 10 min check mistakes and write what you can now explain.

3. Retrieval practice queue

Review from memory before opening notes. Explain the concept aloud, solve a fresh example, then check what you missed.

4. Evidence that proves your skill

Use this checklist before calling a skill portfolio-ready. A certificate or completed video alone is not enough.

These checks are a self-review aid, not an accredited certification or guarantee of job readiness.

5. A simple learning loop that works

Understand

Define the problem, prerequisites and key terms. Ask why the idea works.

Retrieve

Close the notes. Recall the steps, draw the idea or explain it from memory.

Practise

Solve a new problem. Start small, then add realistic constraints and edge cases.

Get feedback

Compare with a trusted answer, run tests or ask a knowledgeable person to review.

Build

Apply the skill to a useful mini-project with a clear output and documented decisions.

Revisit

Review after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days, adjusting when recall is weak.
