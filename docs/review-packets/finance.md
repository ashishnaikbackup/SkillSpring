## Course introduction

Learn to organise money, understand financial trade-offs and evaluate risk. Educational material only; not personalised investment advice.

## Lessons

### 1. Money map & cash flow

**Concept.** Personal finance begins with knowing money coming in, money going out and the timing of both. Separate needs, wants, fixed costs, variable costs and irregular expenses. Track actual spending before setting targets.

**Example**

```
Monthly surplus = total income - total expenses
Savings rate = amount saved / take-home income × 100%
```

**Explanation / note.** A budget is a plan, not a judgement. Use realistic categories and review them regularly. Do not share account numbers, passwords or private statements in public projects.

**Task.** Create a fictional monthly budget with income, essentials, discretionary spending and a savings goal.

**Quiz.** What does a cash-flow budget compare?
- Correct: Money received and money spent over time
- Wrong: Only investment returns
- Wrong: Only account balance

### 2. Banking, interest & inflation

**Concept.** Understand savings accounts, deposits, fees, payment methods and statements. Simple interest is calculated on principal; compound interest earns interest on prior interest. Inflation reduces purchasing power over time.

**Example**

```
Compound value = principal × (1 + rate / periods)^(periods × time)
```

**Explanation / note.** Rates, fees, tax treatment and deposit protections vary by country and product. Compare effective returns after fees and applicable taxes, not only advertised rates.

**Task.** Use a spreadsheet to compare fictional savings scenarios with different rates, time periods and compounding frequencies.

**Quiz.** What is inflation?
- Correct: A general rise in prices that reduces purchasing power
- Wrong: A guaranteed investment return
- Wrong: A bank transfer

### 3. Emergency funds, debt & credit

**Concept.** An emergency fund helps manage unexpected costs without relying on expensive borrowing. Compare debt by interest rate, fees, repayment schedule and consequences of missed payments. Credit reports and lending rules vary by jurisdiction.

**Example**

```
Debt payoff plan: list balances, interest rates, minimum payments and due dates. Compare highest-interest-first and smallest-balance-first approaches.
```

**Explanation / note.** Do not borrow to invest or treat credit limits as income. Contact lenders early if repayment becomes difficult and verify local consumer protections.

**Task.** Create a fictional debt repayment table. Compare two repayment orders and explain the interest-versus-motivation trade-off.

**Quiz.** Why build an emergency fund?
- Correct: To handle unexpected costs without high-cost borrowing
- Wrong: To guarantee investment profits
- Wrong: To avoid tracking spending

### 4. Insurance & risk management

**Concept.** Insurance transfers specified financial risks in exchange for premiums, subject to exclusions, limits and deductibles. Understand what is covered, waiting periods, claims process and renewal terms. Avoid buying based only on marketing.

**Example**

```
Expected loss (simplified) = probability of event × financial impact
```

**Explanation / note.** Insurance is about managing potentially large losses, not making a profit. Read policy wording and verify insurer and intermediary credentials in your jurisdiction.

**Task.** Compare two fictional health or vehicle policy summaries. List coverage, exclusions, limits, premium and questions to ask.

**Quiz.** What should you review before buying insurance?
- Correct: Coverage, exclusions, limits, costs and claims terms
- Wrong: Only the advertisement
- Wrong: Only the brand colour

### 5. Investing basics & diversification

**Concept.** Investing involves risk. Understand asset classes, time horizon, liquidity, volatility, diversification, fees and taxes. Equity, debt, cash-like products, gold and property behave differently. Past performance does not guarantee future results.

**Example**

```
Real return (approx.) = nominal return - inflation rate
```

**Explanation / note.** Risk tolerance and ability to take risk are not the same. Avoid concentrated bets, leverage and products you cannot explain. Verify current regulations and tax rules using official sources.

**Task.** Build a fictional diversified portfolio comparison. Explain how its value might change in a market decline; do not use real money.

**Quiz.** What does diversification aim to reduce?
- Correct: Concentration risk
- Wrong: All market risk
- Wrong: All possibility of loss

### 6. Tax basics, records & fraud safety

**Concept.** Keep organised records: income statements, bank statements, investment statements, insurance documents and receipts. In India, tax rules, slabs, deadlines and deductions change, so rely on the official income tax portal and a qualified professional for your situation; this lesson is education, not tax advice. Know the purpose of your PAN and bank KYC, and review annual statements for errors. Fraud protection matters as much as returns: never share an OTP, UPI PIN, card PIN or password; be suspicious of urgency, prizes and unknown links; verify through the official app or website; and report fraud quickly to your bank and through the national cybercrime channels.

**Example**

```
Personal finance folder (fictional example)
  /income      salary slips, interest statements
  /tax         filed returns, tax statements, proofs
  /banking     account and card statements
  /investments statements and contract notes
  /insurance   policy documents and renewal dates

Monthly 15-minute check
  1. Match transactions to your budget categories
  2. Look for unknown debits or subscriptions
  3. Update the net worth sheet and the renewal calendar
```

**Explanation / note.** A simple structure and a short routine make it easier to file accurately, find proofs and notice unauthorised transactions early.

**Task.** Create a fictional records folder and a monthly check routine. Write a one-page personal fraud checklist that lists what you will never share, where you will verify a message, and whom you will contact if something goes wrong.

**Quiz.** Which of these should you never share with anyone who contacts you?
- Correct: Your OTP or UPI PIN
- Wrong: The name of the bank you use publicly
- Wrong: The general purpose of a savings account

### 7. Investing basics: risk, diversification & costs

**Concept.** This lesson is education, not investment advice. Investments carry risk: returns are not guaranteed and values can fall. Higher possible returns usually come with higher risk. Diversification means not relying on one asset or company. Costs matter because they compound: a small yearly difference in return or fees changes the final amount greatly over many years. Build an emergency fund and clear high-interest debt before taking investment risk, match the time horizon to the goal, and be sceptical of promised guaranteed high returns, which are a common sign of fraud. Check that any adviser or platform is registered with the relevant regulator, such as SEBI in India, and read the official documents.

**Example**

```
Fictional example: 1,00,000 invested for 20 years
  at 7% a year after costs : about 3,86,968
  at 6% a year after costs : about 3,20,714
  Difference from one percentage point: about 66,255

Questions before investing
  What is the goal and the time horizon?
  What can I afford to lose or leave untouched?
  What are the total costs each year?
  Is the seller registered? Where are the official documents?
```

**Explanation / note.** A single percentage point of yearly cost or return changes the outcome by about a fifth over 20 years. The numbers are an illustration of compounding, not a forecast.

**Task.** Make a one-page checklist of questions you will ask before using any investment product. Recalculate the cost example with your own fictional amounts and years, and write three red flags that suggest a scam.

**Quiz.** Which is a common warning sign of an investment scam?
- Correct: A promise of guaranteed high returns with little or no risk
- Wrong: A clear explanation of costs and risks
- Wrong: A seller who is registered with the regulator

### 8. Credit, loans & EMI mathematics

**Concept.** A loan lets you spend now and repay later with interest. An equated monthly instalment, or EMI, is a fixed payment that covers interest and part of the principal each month. Early payments are mostly interest, and later payments are mostly principal. Total interest depends on the rate, the amount and the number of months, so a longer term lowers the EMI but increases total interest. Compare offers by the total amount repaid, fees and the effective rate, and read the lender's repayment schedule and agreement. A credit score summarises repayment history, so paying on time and keeping balances manageable matters. This is education, not advice about any loan.

**Example**

```
EMI formula: EMI = P x r x (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n - 1)
  P = amount borrowed, r = monthly rate (annual rate / 12), n = number of months

Fictional loan: P = 5,00,000, annual rate 10%, n = 60 months
  r = 0.10 / 12 = 0.008333
  EMI = about 10,623.52
  Total repaid = about 6,37,411
  Total interest = about 1,37,411

Checklist: interest rate type (fixed or floating), processing fees, prepayment rules, penalties, total cost
```

**Explanation / note.** The EMI and totals follow from the formula. Compare lenders by total cost and terms, not only the monthly payment.

**Task.** Recalculate the loan for 36, 60 and 84 months. Build a table of EMI, total repaid and total interest, and write two sentences on the trade-off between a lower monthly payment and a higher total cost. Add your own fictional fees.

**Quiz.** What happens to the EMI and the total interest when a loan term is lengthened?
- Correct: The EMI falls but the total interest paid rises
- Wrong: Both the EMI and total interest fall
- Wrong: The EMI rises and total interest falls

### 9. Financial plan & decision project

**Concept.** A useful financial plan connects goals, time horizon, cash needs, risk and review dates. Keep records secure, use trusted institutions and understand fees. Never share OTPs, passwords, seed phrases or remote-access permissions.

**Example**

```
Goal worksheet: goal · target amount · date · monthly contribution · assumptions · risks · review date
```

**Explanation / note.** A calculator is only as good as its assumptions. Show uncertainty with scenarios and avoid promising returns. For complex tax, legal or investment decisions, consult a qualified professional.

**Task.** Capstone: create a fictional personal finance dashboard with a budget, savings goals, debt schedule, insurance checklist and scenario calculator. Use sample numbers only.

**Quiz.** What should a financial projection show?
- Correct: Assumptions, scenarios, risks and limitations
- Wrong: A guaranteed future balance
- Wrong: Only the best-case result
