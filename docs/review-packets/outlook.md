## Course introduction

Manage professional email, calendars, meetings and collaboration with clear, secure workplace habits.

## Lessons

### 1. Email foundations & inbox

**Concept.** Understand addresses, subject lines, To/CC/BCC, attachments, signatures, folders and conversation threads. Email is a record; write for clarity and action.

**Example**

```
Compose a concise email with a descriptive subject, greeting, context, request, deadline and sign-off. Attach a non-sensitive sample file.
```

**Task.** Deliverable: a professional email template and organised inbox folders.

**Quiz.** When should BCC be used?
- Correct: When recipients should not see one another's addresses
- Wrong: To hide the subject
- Wrong: To attach a large file

### 2. Writing, etiquette & follow-up

**Concept.** Use concise paragraphs, one clear ask, appropriate tone and explicit deadlines. Verify recipients and attachments. Avoid replying-all unnecessarily and never send confidential information to the wrong audience.

**Example**

```
Rewrite a vague email into a clear action request. Draft a polite follow-up and an out-of-office reply.
```

**Task.** Create templates for request, update, meeting follow-up and escalation.

**Quiz.** What makes an email actionable?
- Correct: Clear context, owner, requested action and due date
- Wrong: Longer paragraphs
- Wrong: A subject containing only 'Hi'

**Findings:** _none yet_

### 3. Calendar & time management

**Concept.** Create events, invite attendees, use time zones, availability, recurrence and reminders. Include an agenda and meeting purpose. Respect focus time and scheduling boundaries.

**Example**

```
Schedule a fictional project meeting with agenda, attendees, duration, time zone and pre-read.
```

**Task.** Deliverable: a one-week calendar plan with focus blocks and meeting buffers.

**Quiz.** What should a useful meeting invitation include?
- Correct: Purpose, agenda, time, attendees and preparation
- Wrong: Only a title
- Wrong: No time zone for a global team

**Concept.** Learn channels, chats, calls, meeting roles, screen sharing, file collaboration and meeting notes. Keep decisions and actions discoverable in the right team space.

Create a meeting agenda, take notes and publish decisions, action owners and deadlines in a shared location.

**Task.** Prepare a meeting notes template and action tracker.

**Quiz.** Where should durable team decisions be recorded?

### 5. Files, security & privacy

**Concept.** Check sharing permissions, verify recipients, use approved storage and recognise phishing, spoofed senders, suspicious links and unexpected attachments. Follow organisational retention and data rules.

**Example**

```
Review a simulated suspicious email. Identify sender mismatch, urgency, unexpected attachment and unsafe link. Report rather than interact.
```

**Task.** Create a safe email and file-sharing checklist.

**Quiz.** What should you do with a suspicious attachment?
- Correct: Do not open it; report it through the approved security process
- Wrong: Forward it to everyone
- Wrong: Download it to inspect

### 6. Rules, search & shared mailboxes

**Concept.** Rules apply actions automatically to messages that meet conditions, such as moving newsletters to a folder or flagging messages from your manager. Test a rule on a few messages before relying on it, and review rules regularly so important mail is not hidden. Search is faster than browsing: filters such as from:, subject: and hasattachments:yes narrow results. Categories and flags show what needs action. In many organisations, shared mailboxes and delegate access let a team handle a common address; what is available depends on your organisation's settings, so ask your administrator.

**Example**

```
Search examples:
  from:priya subject:invoice
  hasattachments:yes received:last week

Rule idea (fictional):
  When a message arrives from newsletters@example.com
  Then move it to the folder Reading and mark it as read
  Except if my name is in the subject
```

**Explanation / note.** Rules save time but should be tested and reviewed so nothing important is hidden.

**Task.** Create two folders and one rule that files a fictional newsletter automatically. Send yourself three test messages, check the rule works, then write down one risk of automating mail and how you would reduce it.

**Quiz.** What is an inbox rule?
- Correct: An automatic action applied to messages that meet conditions you set
- Wrong: A policy that deletes all email after a week
- Wrong: A way to block every sender at once

### 7. Professional workflow capstone

**Concept.** Combine email, calendar and team collaboration into a clear workflow: request, schedule, meeting, decisions, actions and follow-up.

**Example**

```
Run a simulated project kickoff: send invitation, circulate agenda, record decisions, assign actions and send a concise follow-up.
```

**Task.** Capstone: a complete communication pack with email thread, calendar plan, meeting notes and action log.

**Quiz.** What demonstrates professional communication?
- Correct: Clear, timely, respectful messages with traceable actions
- Wrong: Frequent messages without purpose
- Wrong: Using reply-all for every update
